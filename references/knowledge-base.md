## A. Learning Theory Foundations

1. **Behaviorism** (Watson, 1913; Skinner's operant conditioning, 1938–53) — Learning is a change in observable behavior shaped by stimulus–response associations, reinforcement, and consequences. *Consult when: compliance training, drill & practice, behavior shaping, reinforcement schedules, "observable performance" arguments.*

2. **Cognitivism** (1950s–70s; Miller, Bruner, Neisser; Atkinson & Shiffrin memory model, 1968) — Learning is processing of information through attention, working memory, storage, and retrieval; the mind is modeled, not just behavior. *Consult when: chunking, schema-building, worked examples, memory-friendly sequencing.*

3. **Constructivism** (Piaget's genetic epistemology; von Glasersfeld's radical constructivism) — Learners actively construct knowledge from experience rather than passively receiving it. *Consult when: discovery learning, learner-generated explanations, prior-knowledge activation.*

4. **Social Constructivism / ZPD & Scaffolding** (Vygotsky, "Mind in Society," 1978) — Learning is mediated by social interaction; the Zone of Proximal Development is what a learner can do with support but not yet alone; scaffolding is support that fades. *Consult when: collaborative learning, peer instruction, coaching structures, fading guidance.*

5. **Constructionism** (Papert, "Mindstorms," 1980) — Learning happens most richly when learners build shareable artifacts (code, models, media). *Consult when: project-based learning, maker-style activities, learning-by-making.*

6. **Andragogy — Adult Learning Theory** (Knowles, 1973 onward) — Adults need to know why, bring experience, prefer problem-centered relevance, and thrive on autonomy. Core default for corporate/L&D work. *Consult when: any adult/professional audience design.*

7. **Experiential Learning Theory** (Kolb, 1984) — Four-stage cycle: concrete experience → reflective observation → abstract conceptualization → active experimentation; learning styles derived from it. *Consult when: structuring debriefs, reflection prompts, learning-cycle design (use the cycle; treat the "styles" offshoot with caution).*

8. **Humanistic / Learner-Centered Learning** (Rogers, "Freedom to Learn," 1969; Maslow) — Learning is fostered by authenticity, acceptance, and empathy; the learner's self-direction is central. *Consult when: coaching programs, self-directed learning, facilitator stance and climate.*

9. **Social Learning / Modeling & Self-Efficacy** (Bandura, 1977; "Self-Efficacy," 1997) — People learn by observing models; beliefs about own capability drive effort and persistence. *Consult when: demonstrations, exemplar videos, role models, confidence-building (ties to ARCS "C").*

10. **Situated Learning / Legitimate Peripheral Participation** (Lave & Wenger, 1991) — Learning is embedded in participation in a community of practice, entering peripherally and moving toward full membership. *Consult when: apprenticeships, communities of practice, on-the-job learning design.*

11. **Connectivism** (Siemens, 2005; Downes) — For a networked digital age, learning resides in connections among people, content, and tools; knowing where/whom matters as much as content. *Consult when: MOOCs, social/collaborative digital learning, curation-based design.*

12. **Cognitive Load Theory** (Sweller, 1988) — Working memory is limited; load splits into intrinsic (task complexity), extraneous (poor design), and germane (schema-building). The single most cited theory for instructional efficiency. *Consult when: e-learning, slide design, sequencing complex skills, 4C/ID.*

13. **Dual Coding Theory** (Paivio, 1971) — Information processed verbally and visually uses complementary channels; well-paired visuals + words beat words alone. *Consult when: visuals decisions; underpins Mayer's multimedia principles.*

14. **Self-Regulated Learning & Metacognition** (Flavell, 1979; Zimmerman, 2002) — Learners plan, monitor, and adjust their own learning; instruction can teach these skills explicitly. *Consult when: study-skills training, learning-to-learn modules, self-assessment instruments.*

15. **Memory Science: Spacing, Retrieval Practice, Interleaving** (Ebbinghaus 1885; Roediger & Karpicke 2006; Dunlosky et al. 2013) — Durable learning comes from spaced review, effortful retrieval, and mixed practice rather than massed re-reading. *Consult when: reinforcement plans, knowledge-retention strategy, post-training boosters.*

16. **Gagné's Conditions of Learning (theory)** (Gagné, 1965) — Five domains of learning outcomes — verbal information, intellectual skills, cognitive strategies, motor skills, attitudes — each with distinct internal and external conditions. *Consult when: matching strategy to outcome type; foundation of the Nine Events.*

17. **Cognitive Science Synthesis: "How People Learn" findings** (Bransford, Brown & Cocking, 2000) — Three key findings: prior knowledge shapes learning; deep competence requires organized factual knowledge and conceptual frameworks; metacognition enables self-monitoring. *Consult when: evidence base for almost any design recommendation.*

18. **Neuroscience & Education** (e.g., Goswami's review, 2006, "Neuroscience and education: from research to practice?") — Bridging lab neuroscience to classrooms; mostly confirms cognitive findings, warns against neuromyths. *Consult when: someone asks for "brain-based" design — respond with cognitive science, flag neuromyths.*

## B. Design Models & Frameworks

19. **ADDIE** (roots in 1970s US Armed Forces / Florida State ISD) — Analyze, Design, Develop, Implement, Evaluate; the default macro-process of the field. *Consult when: any course/program-level planning; explain phases and outputs.*

20. **SAM — Successive Approximation Model** (Allen, "Leaving ADDIE for SAM," 2012) — Agile, iterative: Savvy Start → iterate design/develop/evaluate cycles → rollout; prototype early, refine through use. *Consult when: fast timelines, uncertain content, stakeholder iteration, e-learning agile builds.*

21. **Dick & Carey Systems Approach Model** (Dick & Carey, "The Systematic Design of Instruction," 1978; 8th ed. 2015) — Components (goal, analysis, objectives, assessments, strategy, materials, formative + summative evaluation) designed interdependently and revised iteratively. *Consult when: formal, high-stakes, mastery-oriented curriculum; assessment-first sequencing.*

22. **Kemp Design Model** (Morrison, Ross, Kemp & Kalman, "Designing Effective Instruction") — Non-linear, nine-element oval: plan, learners, objectives, assessment, sequencing, strategy, message, delivery, evaluation with continuous revision. *Consult when: projects that loop between phases or need classroom-oriented flexibility.*

23. **Backward Design / Understanding by Design (UbD)** (Wiggins & McTighe, 1998; 2nd ed. 2005) — Stage 1 desired results → Stage 2 acceptable evidence → Stage 3 learning plan; "backward" from evidence. *Consult when: K-12/higher-ed curriculum, unit design, alignment-heavy accreditation contexts.*

24. **Bloom's Taxonomy (Original & Revised)** (Bloom et al., 1956; revised Anderson & Krathwohl, 2001) — Cognitive levels Remember→Understand→Apply→Analyze→Evaluate→Create; the standard verb-alignment tool for objectives and assessments. *Consult when: writing/checking any objective or assessment item.*

25. **Krathwohl's Affective Taxonomy** (Krathwohl, Bloom & Masia, 1964) — Receiving → Responding → Valuing → Organizing → Characterizing; guides attitude/values objectives. *Consult when: culture, safety, ethics, empathy, attitude-change objectives.*

26. **Psychomotor Taxonomies** (Simpson, 1972; Harrow, 1972; Dave, 1970) — Imitation → Manipulation → Precision → Articulation → Naturalization for physical-skill objectives. *Consult when: hands-on/technical/physical skills training.*

27. **Gagné's Nine Events of Instruction** (Gagné, 1965; Gagné, Briggs & Wager, "Principles of Instructional Design") — Gain attention → objectives → recall → content → guidance → practice → feedback → assess → retention/transfer. *Consult when: sequencing any lesson, module, or live session.*

28. **Merrill's First Principles of Instruction** (Merrill, 2002 ETR&D article; "First Principles of Instruction," 2013) — Problem/task-centered, Activation, Demonstration, Application, Integration — principles shared by effective theories. *Consult when: quality-checking any design; pushing back on content dumps.*

29. **ARCS Model of Motivational Design** (Keller, 1987) — Attention, Relevance, Confidence, Satisfaction, with a 10-step motivational design process. *Consult when: engagement problems, dropout risk, mandatory/boring-topic training.*

30. **Dale's Cone of Experience** (Dale, "Audio-Visual Methods in Teaching," 1946) — Continuum from abstract (symbols) to direct (purposeful) experience guiding media/activity choice. Caution: the numbered retention percentages circulating with it are fabricated — use directionality only. *Consult when: choosing activity/media formats; matching format to skill depth.*

31. **Kirkpatrick's Four Levels of Evaluation** (Kirkpatrick, 1959; "New World Kirkpatrick Model," Kirkpatrick & Kirkpatrick, 2016) — Reaction, Learning, Behavior, Results; plan evaluation backwards from Level 4. *Consult when: any evaluation design or "how will we know it worked" question.*

32. **Phillips ROI Methodology** (Phillips, 1990s–2000s) — Adds Level 5 (ROI: monetary benefits vs. cost, with isolation and data conversion) on top of Kirkpatrick. *Consult when: L&D budget justification, executive reporting.*

33. **5E Instructional Model** (Bybee et al., BSCS, 1987) — Engage, Explore, Explain, Elaborate, Evaluate; inquiry-based constructivist cycle. *Consult when: STEM sessions, workshop formats, inquiry learning.*

34. **Cognitive Apprenticeship** (Collins, Brown & Newman, 1989) — Modeling → Coaching → Scaffolding & Fading → Articulation → Reflection → Exploration; making expert thinking visible. *Consult when: complex skill training, expert-novice gap, think-aloud demonstrations.*

35. **Mayer's Multimedia Learning Principles** (Mayer, "Multimedia Learning," 2001; expanded editions) — Evidence-based set: coherence, signaling, redundancy, spatial contiguity, temporal contiguity, segmenting, pre-training, modality (narrate vs. read), multimedia, personalization, voice, image. *Consult when: e-learning, video, slides, infographics, any visual+verbal design.*

36. **4C/ID — Four-Component Instructional Design** (van Merriënboer, 1997) — Whole-task practice with declining support + supportive information + procedural guidance + part-task practice, for complex cognitive skills. *Consult when: complex professional skills (medicine, engineering, troubleshooting).*

37. **Rapid Prototyping Model** (Tripp & Bichelmeyer, 1990) — Rapidly build-and-test working instruction chunks before full development; origins of agile ID. *Consult when: SAM ancestry, low-cost iteration contexts.*

38. **Universal Design for Learning (UDL)** (CAST; Rose & Meyer, 2000s) — Multiple means of engagement, representation, and action/expression; accessibility by design. *Consult when: accessibility, inclusive classrooms, varied learner needs.*

39. **Action Mapping** (Moore, 2008; "Map It," 2018) — Start from business measure → behaviors needed → practice activities → minimal content. *Consult when: corporate performance-focused design, cutting content bloat.*

40. **70-20-10 Framework** (Lombardo & Eichinger, Center for Creative Leadership, 1990s) — Development roughly via experience (70), others/coaching (20), formal courses (10); a planning heuristic, not precise data. *Consult when: blended leadership development, on-the-job learning strategy.*

41. **Team-Based Learning (TBL)** (Michaelsen, 2004; 2013 for health professions) — Readiness assurance (individual + team tests) then application exercises in permanent teams. *Consult when: flipping classrooms, group accountability design.*

42. **Fink's Taxonomy of Significant Learning** (Fink, 2003; 2nd ed. 2013) — Six kinds: foundational knowledge, application, integration, human dimension, caring, learning-how-to-learn; integrated course design. *Consult when: higher-ed course (re)design beyond cognitive objectives.*

43. **Understanding by Design's Six Facets of Understanding** (Wiggins & McTighe) — Explain, interpret, apply, have perspective, empathize, self-knowledge. *Consult when: defining "understanding" as assessable evidence.*

## D. Common Myths to Avoid (do not repeat; correct gently if cited by users)

- **Learning styles (VAK/VARK)** — matching teaching to "styles" has no supporting evidence. Design for modality appropriateness and cognitive load instead.
- **The Cone of Learning retention percentages** ("people remember 10% of what they read…") — fabricated numbers attached to Dale's Cone. Use the abstract→direct continuum, never the percentages.
- **Learning pyramid (NTL)** — same family of unfounded retention figures.
- **"We use only 10% of our brain" / Brain Gym / left-right brain learners** — neuromyths; decline politely and use cognitive science.
- **"Digital natives" as a learner category** — age does not confer tech fluency; assess actual skills.
- **70-20-10 as empirical fact** — useful planning heuristic, not measurement data; say so when using it.

## 🏛️ Real World Instructional Design Framework (Cennamo & Kalk, 2019)

Berdasarkan *Real World Instructional Design: An Iterative Approach to Designing Learning Experiences (2nd Edition)* karya Katherine Cennamo & Debby Kalk (Routledge, 2019), praktik instruksional di lapangan diatur oleh prinsip-prinsip operasional berikut:

### 1. The Essential Triangle of Instructional Design
* **Tiga Sudut Penyelarasan**: **Outcomes** (apa yang ingin dicapai), **Assessments** (bagaimana mengukur ketercapaian), dan **Activities** (pengalaman untuk mencapai hasil).
* **Pusat Segitiga**: **Learners** (Pembelajar berada di titik pusat seluruh keputusan desain).
* **Pembungkus**: **Evaluation** (Evaluasi membungkus ketiga sudut untuk memastikan keselarasan dan relevansi terus-menerus).
* **Prinsip Masuk Fleksibel (*Non-Linear Entry*)**: Perancang tidak wajib mulai dari objektif! Perancang dapat mulai dari:
  * *Assessment-First*: Sangat efektif saat bekerja dengan SME lapangan yang kesulitan menyusun objektif teoritis tapi paham betul seperti apa tes/standar kelayakan kerja.
  * *Activity/Content-First*: Saat klien sudah memiliki materi/studi kasus yang kaya dan ingin menstrukturkannya ke dalam kurikulum.
  * *Outcomes-First*: Pendekatan klasik saat kesenjangan kinerja terdefinisi jelas.
  * **Kunci Utama**: Dari mana pun masuknya, ketiga sudut segitiga wajib berada dalam kondisi selaras (*aligned*).

### 2. The 5-Phase Knowledge-Building Spiral
Bukan proses linier kaku "waterfall", melainkan gerakan spiral dari dalam ke luar:
1. **Define Phase**: Menentukan kebutuhan instruksional, profil awal pembelajar, proposal proyek, asumsi risiko, dan klausul Out-of-Scope (OOS).
2. **Design Phase**: Menyusun dokumen desain (*Design Document*), peta alur (*Flowchart*), dan dokumen materi (*Content Document*) sebelum menyentuh produksi media.
3. **Demonstrate Phase**: Membangun prototipe konseptual (*Treatment*, skenario naratif, template layar) dan prototipe kerja (*working prototype / slice*) untuk diuji coba ke pembelajar (*learner tryout*).
4. **Develop Phase**: Produksi penuh seluruh aset media, panduan fasilitator/peserta, dan jaminan mutu (QA).
5. **Deliver Phase**: Peluncuran, pelatihan klien/trainer, debriefing *postmortem*, kalkulasi indeks biaya (*instructional cost index*), dan rekomendasi Version 2 (V2).

### 3. The Collaborative ASC Cycle (Assemble, Synthesize, Check)
Setiap fase digerakkan oleh siklus kolaboratif 3 langkah:
* **Assemble & Ask**: Mengumpulkan informasi dari klien, mewawancarai SME, meneliti dokumen sumber.
* **Synthesize & Solve**: Mengolah informasi ke dalam solusi tentatif (outline, flowchart, treatment, storyboard).
* **Confirm & Check**: Memverifikasi pemahaman dengan stakeholder, menguji coba dengan pembelajar, merevisi berdasarkan umpan balik.

### 4. Zahorik's 4 Instructional Sequencing Models (1995)
Memilih sekuens pembelajaran berdasarkan keteraturan (Hierarki Langsung vs Berpusat Masalah) dan sifat hasil (Konvergen vs Divergen):
1. **Application Model** (Hierarki Langsung + Konvergen): Sederhana ke kompleks menuju jawaban/prosedur baku yang telah ditentukan (Aktifkan pengetahuan awal -> Peroleh skill -> Latihan terbimbing -> Refleksi).
2. **Discovery Model** (Berpusat Masalah + Konvergen): Memecahkan masalah terbimbing menuju prinsip baku tertentu (Eksperimen variabel lift/hambatan -> Menemukan hukum fisika -> Refleksi).
3. **Extension Model** (Hierarki Langsung + Divergen): Menguasai keterampilan hierarkis untuk menghasilkan karya orisinal/terbuka (Menguasai struktur paragraf -> Menulis esai persuasif orisinal).
4. **Invention Model** (Berpusat Masalah + Divergen): Pembelajar diceburkan ke dalam masalah kompleks terbuka dengan banyak alternatif solusi (Tantangan rancang bangun prototipe -> Coaching fasilitator -> Refleksi).

### 5. Analisis Pembelajar: Contrived vs. Derived
* **Contrived Analysis**: Analisis dugaan terbaik berbasis pengalaman tim pengembang, curah pendapat dengan klien, dan kajian literatur ketika akses langsung ke pembelajar terbatas.
* **Derived Analysis**: Analisis berbasis data empiris yang diperoleh langsung dari perwakilan pembelajar melalui wawancara, kuesioner, observasi langsung, atau *focus group*.

### 6. Tabel Revisi Formatif (*Revision Table*)
Evaluasi formatif (One-on-One, Pilot Test, Field Trial) dirangkum dalam tabel tindakan yang dapat diaudit:
* **Komponen**: Layar / Unit / Modul yang dievaluasi.
* **Masalah**: Kesulitan atau kesalahpahaman yang dialami pembelajar.
* **Bukti & Sumber**: Kutipan umpan balik, data salah jawab kuis, observasi fasilitator.
* **Rencana Perubahan**: Tindakan perbaikan konkret (in-scope vs out-of-scope).
